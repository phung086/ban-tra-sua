# M2.2 / QR-05 — Isolate oversized geometry from culling cells

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Baseline branch SHA:** `6d260e33f95258a57b0786a4638e00b052d2bae9`. **Task:** QR-05 (M2, spatial culling, step 2). **Previous CI:** [#38029853901](https://github.com/phung086/ban-tra-sua/actions/runs/38029853901) success at baseline SHA. **Baseline production:** [#38029853908](https://github.com/phung086/ban-tra-sua/actions/runs/38029853908) was still in progress when this patch was prepared; do not invent metrics.

## Root cause / reproducible scene

`mergeRigid` previously grouped meshes only by material and **origin position's sector**, regardless of the world-space footprint. A 100-unit road/wall and three 1-unit props with the same material and sector could be welded into one geometry with a huge bounding sphere. When the road is in the camera frustum but the small props are outside, the merged geometry forces the props' triangles through the visible render and shadow passes. This defeats the intent of spatial culling and risks wasted follow-camera work.

## One scoped implementation

In `src/scene/batching.ts`, for **sector-based** merges only, use the geometry's cached local bounding box transformed by `mesh.matrixWorld`. If its horizontal AABB width or depth exceeds the applicable near/far sector size, leave the original mesh in the scene instead of welding it to unrelated details. Small meshes continue to merge exactly as before. No distance-based hard disappearance, no new LOD threshold, no change to NPC, vehicle, interaction collider, quest, save, quality tier, or streaming. AABB is conservative under rotation; this deliberately favors correctness over maximum batching.

In `src/scene/batching.test.ts`, add a regression with an oversized 100-unit road and three props in the same 32-unit cell. Assert: road remains a separate mesh; props merge; exact world-space bounding box preserved; a camera can see the road while frustum-culling the offscreen merged props. Existing tests still cover moving subtrees, sector boundaries, transformed roots and instancing.
