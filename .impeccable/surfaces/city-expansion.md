Mode: Experience in the street; Operate for navigation, dialogue and errands. This user-authorized expansion follows world-foundation and supersedes its no-new-gameplay boundary. The user urgently requests a larger city, much richer detail, fun missions, friendly/joking/brusque answers and characters with difficult personalities. Preserve tea-shop focus, original saves, mobile round-stick control, warm-paper/rose UI and seed e0aa4a37.

## TYPE
Be Vietnam Pro. Dialogue choices name both the answer and its tone; controls remain 44px. More content scrolls inside the notebook/dialogue rather than obscuring the street by default.

## MATERIAL
Actual interactive 3D geometry with authored albedo and normal materials for stone, brick, asphalt, bark, roof tiles and fabric. Organic trees have branching trunks, roots, canopy volume and instanced leaf silhouettes. House façades, rooflines, doors and districts vary. People retain articulated limbs, individual looks, facial geometry, garment seams and hair detail. Paving, asphalt, bark and roof materials use bundled CC0 photographs with normal maps, prepared at 512px for GPU use. People and buildings retain procedural geometry; do not claim photorealistic characters or real-building asset parity.

## GROUND
Warm pink paper controls; natural weathered materials for the world. Paving mortar and asphalt aggregate belong to the street's physical surfaces. Shared material/geometry budgets and sector batching carry detail without a separate draw per brick or leaf.

## THESIS
A tea-shop owner lives within a connected Hanoi-inspired city. Four additional districts have actual walkable destinations, roads and authored errands. Six personalities remember conversation tone and unlock three story chapters, with choices between different jobs. Completing work changes rapport and supports the tea shop.

## FIRST VIEWPORT / FORM

The notebook separates the local map from the city map so pins remain usable. Existing complete quests and saves remain valid; reward and chapter guards remain enforced.

Physical phone performance remains a separate unverified test; photorealism is an aspiration rather than a shipped claim.
